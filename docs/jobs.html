What this shows

  • Value and jobs have come apart. Walmart alone has 42% of the group's jobs with 2% of its profit. Nvidia, SpaceX and Broadcom together have 2% of the jobs with 21% of the profit and 25% of the market value.
  • The gap between the ends is several hundred times. Ford has about 3,660 jobs per $1 billion of market value, Nvidia about 11 and Anthropic about 5. Nvidia earns $4.59 million of net profit per employee, Walmart about $11,000.
  • High margin does not always mean few jobs. Microsoft and Alphabet have net margins above 40% and still employ more than 200,000 people each. Amazon is both a top-five company by value and the second largest employer in the group.
  • Estimate: moving 10% of the group's profit into wages would pay about 1.1 million average US jobs. That is $108 billion a year at the average US employer cost of $97,500 per full-time job. The gain in federal tax would be small, about $6 billion to $19 billion a year: the main change is who receives the money, not how much tax is paid.
  • AI companies sit at the far end. Anthropic reports $6.6 million of revenue per employee with 5,300 staff. Its yearly revenue equals the employer cost of about 360,000 average US jobs. So far a study of US data finds no clear rise in unemployment in the jobs most exposed to AI, but hiring of young workers in those jobs has slowed by 14%.

The estimates use stated assumptions and are not measurements. From the article Trillion-dollar companies: jobs, margins and what comes back to the US economy. Data file: trillion-companies-2026-11-20.csv.
