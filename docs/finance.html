What does the frontier cost, and what does it earn?

Anthropic's IPO filing is the first set of audited accounts from a frontier lab. In six quarters, revenue per dollar of compute rose from $0.40 to $2.08, at least 73% of compute went to something other than serving customers, and the company signed about $853 billion of commitments, almost all for compute.

Future obligations and remaining contracts: what they mean

The filing uses several similar words for money that is promised but not yet moved. They point in opposite directions.

Remaining contracts (remaining performance obligations, $10.8B): money customers have agreed to pay Anthropic under non-cancelable contracts, for service it has not delivered yet. It is future revenue that is already signed. It does not include pay-as-you-go usage with no commitment, so it is small next to the $16.3B of revenue earned in the first half of 2026 alone.

Future obligations (purchase commitments, $265.6B, and leases not yet started, $321.4B): money Anthropic has agreed to pay cloud and chip providers such as Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Broadcom and others. They are future costs that are already signed, and most cannot be cancelled. New deals signed from July 1 to October 26, 2026 add about $266B.

Deferred revenue ($1.65B) is a third item: cash customers already paid for service not yet delivered. It is part of the $10.8B.

Read together: the first is what is coming in on signed contracts, the others are what is going out. Today the money going out on contracts is about 79 times the money coming in on contracts, which is why the filing leans on revenue growth to cover it.

How to read this

Four things to take away

  • The $80.7B net loss is mostly not cash. $72.4B of it is the rising value of convertible notes that are owed in shares. The operating loss for the first half of 2026 was $0.7B, and Q2 2026 made an operating profit of $974M.
  • Revenue per dollar of compute is rising fast. The direction is clear. The size is less certain, because part of the gain is timing: compute is bought 12 to 18 months ahead.
  • Training, not serving, is the big cost. The split is the company's own allocation and a lower bound.
  • Most of the commitments are payable whether or not the capacity is used. The $853B is LLMeter's sum of the parts the filing discloses.

Where the numbers come from

  • As printed in the filing: every number not in italics and not marked *.
  • Calculated by LLMeter: numbers in italics or marked *: the ratios, gross margins, unit economics, Q3 2026 revenue and the commitment sum. All of them, with page references, are in anthropic-s1-2026-10-26.csv.

What is missing

  • This first version of the filing leaves Q3 2026 revenue, run-rate revenue, the total commitment figure and the offering price blank. The amended filing has not been read.
  • It gives dollars only: no gigawatts, chip counts, model sizes or training-run costs.
