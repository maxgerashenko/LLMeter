What this shows

  • Predicting several tokens at once is worth 2x to 3.6x. In speculative decoding a small draft model, or extra prediction heads, guesses several tokens that the large model then checks in one pass. The original method gives identical outputs.
  • Diffusion models are the outlier. The 10x figure is the developer's own comparison against other companies' models, so quality is not held equal.
  • Attention and memory engineering give 2x to 6x each. FlashAttention, paged KV-cache memory with continuous batching and latent attention all attack the same bottleneck: memory traffic, not arithmetic.
  • Quantization speedups depend on hardware. The 2022 result was 3.25x to 4.5x. A 2024 study on current GPUs found 1.08x to 3.09x, largest on the biggest model.
  • These gains stack only partly. They compete for the same headroom, and no source read here measures the full stack, so the percentages must not be multiplied together.

What this shows

  • Reasoning is the largest single lever of the last two years. The cleanest measurement is one base model with reasoning training added: its AIME 2024 score doubles, while general knowledge (MMLU) moves by about 3%.
  • The harness matters as much as the model on agent tasks. With the model held fixed, a better agent interface raised the share of SWE-bench Lite tasks resolved from 11.0% to 18.0%.
  • Distillation moves most of the gain into a small model. A 32B model distilled from a reasoning model reaches 91% of its score with under 5% of its parameters.
  • Efficiency techniques are nearly free in quality. Quantizing to 8 or 4 bits keeps 96% to 101% of the full-precision score.
  • Thinking is not always a gain. In LLMeter's local runs, turning thinking on for two small models under fixed token and time limits lowered the score by 24% to 64%.
