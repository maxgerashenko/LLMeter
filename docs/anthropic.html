What does the frontier cost, and what does it earn?

Anthropic's IPO filing is the first set of audited accounts from a frontier lab. In six quarters, revenue per dollar of compute rose from $0.40 to $2.08, at least 73% of compute went to something other than serving customers, and the company signed about $853 billion of commitments, almost all for compute.

The Q3 2025 loss includes a $1.5 billion copyright settlement. Compute is contracted 12 to 18 months ahead and expensed when it comes online, so part of the improvement is demand growing into capacity already bought. The company warns that margins may fall again when the next large blocks arrive.

Q3 2026 revenue is blank in this filing. It states that the quarter was 86 times Q4 2024 revenue of $197 million, which gives about $16.9 billion, up about 47% on Q2 2026 after 132% and 144% in the two quarters before.

2023 to 2025 are audited; the half years are not. "Compute outside cost of revenue" covers research, training the next models, Anthropic's own use of Claude, and free access, so it is not a pure training figure. It is a lower bound because cost of revenue also holds some staff and overhead costs.

Serving is the small part: at most $2.4 billion to serve $16.3 billion of revenue in the first half of 2026. The training cycle is the large part, and it recurs: model versions released in the previous six months produced over 80% of Q3 2026 revenue (p. 143).

How to read this

  • The $80.7 billion net loss for the first half of 2026 is mostly accounting. $72.4 billion of it is the rising value of convertible notes that are owed in shares, not cash. The operating loss was $693 million, and Q2 2026 made an operating profit of $974 million.
  • Compute efficiency is now measurable. Confidence: high for the direction, medium for the size, because part of the improvement is timing.
  • The cost of the frontier is the training cycle, not serving. Confidence: medium. The split is the company's own allocation and a lower bound.
  • Progress is being bought forward. Confidence: medium. The $853 billion is LLMeter's sum of the disclosed parts, and most of it is payable whether or not the capacity is used.
