How much expert work can a model do on its own?

METR measures the time horizon: how long a task is, in hours an expert human needs for it, when the AI agent finishes it 50% of the time. Below, that is mapped to a working day and week, followed by the success curve from 50% up to 100%.

1. Expert work time at 50% success

Each bar is the task length (in expert human time) a model completes half of the time, as published by METR. Dashed lines are plain arithmetic: one 8-hour working day and one 40-hour working week.

    3. From 50% to 100% success: the reliability curve

    Success rate as a function of task length (expert human time, log scale). Every curve starts near 100% on very short tasks and reaches 50% at the model's time horizon. The curves are derived from METR's two published points (50% and 80%) with a logistic in log task length; they are not METR's own plotted curves.

    METR does not publish a 99% horizon: it says measuring it would need many more, very short tasks, and results would be sensitive to methodology. The 90% and 95% columns are extrapolations from the two published points. Treat them as estimates.

    What is sourced and what is assumed

    Sourced: every model's 50% and 80% horizon, release date and the doubling time, from METR's data file (METR-Horizon-v1.1). Data file here: metr-time-horizons-1-1.csv.

    Derived by LLMeter: the curves, and the 90% and 95% lengths, from the two published points.

    Assumption, not data: the "senior developer with 8 years of experience who knows the codebase". METR's human baseliners had about 5 years of relevant experience and little or no context for the task, and METR warns that a time horizon is not "hours of work a high-context professional does in their day-to-day job". No public source gives the speed-up for a developer who knows the codebase, so the slider starts at 1× (METR's own units). If you set it to 3×, a task METR counts as 3 hours is treated as 1 hour for that developer, so the model's horizon in that developer's time shrinks to a third.

    Not covered: METR has no Claude Sonnet 5.x or Fable 5.x, and no open-weights model, so none of those can be placed on this page. This list is a selection of METR's entries from 2023 on, not all of them.
